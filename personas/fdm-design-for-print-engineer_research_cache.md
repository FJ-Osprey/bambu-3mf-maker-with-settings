### Drilling verdict

Three separate questions:

(1) Drilling a hole that was never printed. Sources that say anything treat it as an accepted option, but none in my fetched set recommends it as the default for a hole you can print. CNC Kitchen tested drilled holes only to calibrate: "I printed a second full set of samples and drilled the holes to their exact nominal diameter", and suggests "If you're a real perfectionist, you could even drill the holes to size before installing the inserts" (CNCK-DS). Rahix and JU treat drilling as the way to open a sacrificial bridge, not as the way to make a hole. The consensus I found for new holes is to print them (with the compensation above), and drill only the bridge layer.

(2) Reaming or drilling out a printed hole to final size. This is where the sources lean toward post-process, with the opposing view stated:
- For: "For fits and bearing seats, always drill or ream" (FormHub, search snippet only, not fetched); "print an undersized pilot and drill it out... A drilled hole in a printed part is round, straight and on-size in a way a printed one never quite is" and "A genuine bearing seat -> print undersized and ream" (SYNTAX, fetched). Rahix: "Reaming printed holes to an H6 fit is possible. This option only makes sense where absolutely required" and tight tolerances "can be reached by reaming, not by printing" (RAHIX, fetched). The RepRap forum: print about 0.3 mm small, then ream with a reamer, not a drill bit, because "A drillbit will force itself in the obstruction, often widening the hole in the wrong direction" (REPRAP, fetched, 2017, single forum user).
- Against: a RepRap poster argues you should "make the necessary adjustments to the design of the model itself" rather than post-process; another objected that drilling only helps round holes ("It's any shape. Makes for a lot of filing"). Bambu's own slicer feature (hole compensation, auto circle compensation) exists to avoid post-processing. I did not find a source that measured drilling versus printing for strength or accuracy.
Hubs' FDM design guide PDF reportedly says "When tight tolerances are required, holes will be drilled or reamed" but I could not read that PDF (it came back as unreadable binary); the quote is from a search snippet only.

(3) Tapping a printed hole. CNC Kitchen tapped M3 holes in PLA (163 kg pull-out; CNCK-RESIN) and found tapping beat bare screwing in resin; he calls it the middle option. Rahix says tapped plastic threads "do not like repeated assembly". No fetched source says do not tap; none measured reassembly after tapping.

Net: not a contested "never drill printed parts". Nobody says it is wrong. The split is about default: print holes in (consensus for screw/insert holes), ream only for bearing/press/dowel seats where a couple of tenths matter.

### 1. Snap-fit formulas (primary manuals)

Primary source for all of this section: Bayer MaterialScience, "Snap-Fit Joints for Plastics - A Design Guide" (26 pp), PDF copy https://fab.cba.mit.edu/classes/S62.12/people/vernelle.noel/Plastic_Snap_fit_design.pdf [F] (text extracted locally with pdftotext; Covestro host copy: https://solutions.covestro.com/-/media/covestro/solution-center/brands/downloads/imported/1556891135.pdf [X]). Note: Table 1 itself is an image in the PDF, so its rows were not machine-readable. Constants for design 1 come from the secondary sources cited in the rows and from the worked example on p.15.

Notation (Bayer): y = deflection (= undercut), ε = strain at root outer fiber (absolute, i.e. %/100), l = arm length, h = thickness at root, b = width, Es = secant modulus (strain-dependent), P = deflection force, μ = friction, α = lead angle.

### Gaps

1. **No measured permissible strain for printed PLA or PETG.** Every printed-material strain number above is a blog/vendor rule of thumb (PLA 1-8%, PETG 3-5%). No Formlabs/Protolabs/Hubs/Prusa/CNC Kitchen page gave test data for cantilever snaps. Needed: a coupon series (fixed arm, vary deflection) in the project's own filament and orientation.
2. **Bayer Table 1 constants** came from secondary agreement (Covestro brochure, ACADIA paper), not from reading the table image. The 1.09 and p.15 force formulas were read directly. The tapered-width (design 3) and U/L shapes constants were not extracted.
3. **CNC Kitchen's original perimeters-vs-infill and infill-pattern videos, and the printed-layer-height hook test, were not opened;** I relied on secondary summaries (3dprinting.com, printpal, reddit). The temperature test was a transcript extract.
4. **Most sources marked [X] came back as long search-tool page extracts and were not independently opened.** They need an actual fetch if any number goes into a design rule.
5. **No creep/relaxation numbers** for a deflected printed snap arm (percent force lost vs hours, PLA vs PETG). Qualitative only (mechanics.ju.se, thrinter).
6. **No cycle-life data**; PLA estimates differ 10x (5-20 vs 50-200).
7. **Nothing measured on the A1 mini** except Bambu's own material guidance. No A1 mini PETG Z-strength, no measured hole shrink, no hinge-gap test.
8. **No numbers for** dovetail clearance and angle beyond a 7-10 deg flare, bayonet slot geometry, keyed/poka-yoke fits, torsion snap dimensions, annular snap strain for printed rings (only qualitative: print small rings flat, under ~30 mm).
9. **Press-fit interference amounts conflict by 2-4x** because sources mix per-side and diameter conventions; the only safe way is a coupon.
10. **Friction coefficient for printed PLA/PETG on itself** not found (Bayer Table 3 has no PLA/PETG).
11. **Magnet pause command for the A1 mini** and Bambu Studio "insert pause" behavior not verified.
